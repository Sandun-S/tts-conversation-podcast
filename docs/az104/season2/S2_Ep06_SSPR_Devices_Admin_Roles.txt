<Person1> "Welcome back. Episode six: password reset, devices, and administrator roles. We cover self service password reset with its administrator exception, device settings and joins, the difference between Microsoft Entra roles and Azure roles, and the user requirements from the Ironclad case study. Questions thirty nine, forty, forty one, seventy nine and eighty one are our anchors."
</Person1><Person2> "Start with self service password reset."
</Person2><Person1> "Self service password reset, SSPR, lets users reset or unlock their own accounts without calling the help desk. You enable it for none, a selected group, or all users. You choose which authentication methods are available: mobile app notification, mobile app code, email, mobile phone text or call, office phone, and security questions. You choose how many methods are required to reset, one or two. And users must register their methods first, in a combined registration experience. For synchronized users, SSPR needs password writeback through Entra Connect, otherwise the new password never reaches Active Directory. Licensing: SSPR for cloud users is available in the free tier for basic features, but writeback and many controls need Microsoft Entra ID P one or P two."
</Person1><Person2> "Now the security questions detail."
</Person2><Person1> "Security questions have two numbers. How many questions a user must register, from three to five. And how many they must answer correctly at reset, from three to five. You also create or choose the question list: predefined ones like What is your favorite food, or custom questions. Here's the subtlety: if you configure only three questions in total and require three to register and three to answer, then every user is asked all three questions at reset, because there's nothing else to choose from. If the pool were ten questions and the user registered five, they'd be asked a random subset."
</Person1><Person2> "Let's do question forty one then."
</Person2><Person1> "The setup: tenant contoso dot com, synchronized with on premises Active Directory. SecAdmin one has the Security Administrator role. BillAdmin one has the Billing Administrator role. User one has the Reports Reader role. SSPR is enabled for all. Two methods required, mobile phone and security questions available. Three questions registered, three answered. Three questions exist. Question forty one: during reset, User one must answer What was the name of your first pet? True. User one is treated as a regular user under the standard policy. Since three are required and the pool has exactly three, each question is asked, including the pet question. You answered correctly."
</Person1><Person2> "And the administrators, questions thirty nine and forty."
</Person2><Person1> "Question thirty nine: SecAdmin one must answer In what city was your first job? Answer: False. You said True. Question forty: BillAdmin one must answer What is your favorite food? Answer: False. You got this right. The rule: accounts that hold administrator roles are subject to a different SSPR policy, the administrator reset policy. SSPR is enabled for administrators by default, regardless of how the tenant setting is configured. They always need two methods to reset, a so called two gate policy. And the methods allowed for admins do not include security questions. Admins can use email, mobile phone, office phone, and the authenticator app or tokens. So even though the question states that security questions are configured for users, administrators are never asked them."
</Person1><Person2> "Why does Microsoft treat admins differently?"
</Person2><Person1> "Because security questions are weak, answers can be guessed or found on social media. An attacker who can reset an administrator's password owns the tenant. So admins are forced to stronger methods. The exam trick is that the global configuration looks like it applies to everyone, while the admin exception silently overrides it. Memory sentence: admin roles, two gates, no security questions."
</Person1><Person2> "Which roles count as administrator roles here?"
</Person2><Person1> "Most of the privileged Entra roles: Global Administrator, Security Administrator, Billing Administrator, Helpdesk Administrator, User Administrator, Exchange Administrator, SharePoint Administrator and similar. The exam treats Reports Reader as not an administrator role, so User one follows the normal policy. When the exam names a role and you're unsure, ask: does the role name contain administrator? That's the exam's working heuristic."
</Person1><Person2> "Twists on SSPR?"
</Person2><Person1> "They might ask: which users must register? Those in scope for SSPR. They might say SSPR is enabled for a selected group only, and the administrators are not in the group, they still get SSPR by default. Or ask: what do you need so a synchronized user's reset takes effect on premises? Password writeback. Or ask about reducing the number of methods required for a user from two to one: the tenant setting for users, but the admin policy remains two."
</Person1><Person2> "Moving on to devices. Question eighty one."
</Person2><Person1> "First, the vocabulary again, with an example. Anna buys her own phone and wants to read company email: that's registering the device with Entra, a bring your own device scenario. Ironclad gives Anna a corporate laptop, she signs in with her work account, the laptop belongs to the organization: that's Entra join. And the old style laptop that is a member of the on premises Active Directory domain and also known to Entra: hybrid join. The device settings page in Entra, under Devices, Device settings, has these controls. Setting one: Users may join devices to Microsoft Entra, with options All, Selected, None. Setting two: Additional local administrators on Microsoft Entra joined devices. Setting three: Users may register their devices with Microsoft Entra. Setting four: Require multifactor authentication to register or join devices. Setting five: Maximum number of devices per user, default fifty. And also local administrator settings and Enterprise State Roaming."
</Person1><Person2> "And the two requirements in Ironclad?"
</Person2><Person1> "One: only members of a group named DevicePilot may join devices to Entra. Two: when users join devices to Entra, they must verify identity by using a mobile phone. So for requirement one, set Users may join devices to Microsoft Entra from All to Selected, and choose the DevicePilot group. For requirement two, set Require multifactor authentication to join devices from No to Yes. In the exam list those were option one and option four. You selected one and three, so you got one right, and picked register instead of the MFA setting."
</Person1><Person2> "Why isn't register part of it?"
</Person2><Person1> "Register is a different action, for personal devices. The requirement says join. If the exam wanted to restrict personal registration, it would say register. Also the requirement about verifying identity by mobile phone points to multi factor authentication: a text message or call to a phone is a classic second factor. The maximum devices per user setting is a number limit, no requirement mentions it. Additional local administrators is about who is admin on joined devices, no requirement mentions it."
</Person1><Person2> "Is the MFA setting still the modern way?"
</Person2><Person1> "In current Entra, Microsoft recommends a Conditional Access policy targeting the user action Register or join devices, which replaces the old checkbox. But the exam uses the setting named Require multifactor authentication to join devices. In a newer question, if both appear, the Conditional Access approach is considered the supported way, and the legacy device setting is being phased out. Know both. Also note that Conditional Access needs Entra ID P one."
</Person1><Person2> "Twists?"
</Person2><Person1> "Allow only a pilot group to join, selected group. Block joins entirely, none. Allow users to join but make the first user a local admin: that's default behavior, the user who joins becomes local administrator unless you change it with Windows Autopilot or device settings. Limit users to five devices: maximum number of devices."
</Person1><Person2> "Now question seventy nine, and the admin roles."
</Person2><Person1> "The Ironclad requirement: a newly created user named CloudAdmin must be assigned as the service administrator for the Azure subscription. The question: what should you do? Answer: from the Subscriptions blade, select the subscription, and modify the Access control, IAM, settings. Let me give you the history so the wording makes sense. Old Azure had classic subscription administrators: the Account Administrator, the Service Administrator, and Co Administrators. These roles were separate from the modern role based access control. In the old portal, you assigned them on the subscription under classic administrators. Microsoft retired classic administrators in August twenty twenty four, converting Service Administrator and Co Administrators into Owner role assignments at the subscription scope. So in the modern world, making CloudAdmin the service administrator means assigning Owner on the subscription through IAM. The exam answer, IAM on the subscription, matches that."
</Person1><Person2> "And the other options?"
</Person2><Person1> "From the Entra ID blade, modify properties: that's tenant level stuff like name, country, technical contact. From the subscription properties: you can view or rename, but can't assign administrators. From the Entra ID blade, modify groups: group management doesn't give subscription access. Pattern: subscription level access goes through the subscription's own IAM page."
</Person1><Person2> "Entra roles versus Azure roles. I always confuse them."
</Person2><Person1> "Two separate permission systems. Microsoft Entra roles control the directory: users, groups, domains, devices, applications, sign in policies. Examples: Global Administrator, User Administrator, Security Administrator, Billing Administrator, Application Administrator, Attribute Definition Administrator. They are assigned in the Entra admin center, with scope the tenant, an administrative unit, or an app. Azure roles, Azure RBAC, control Azure resources: virtual machines, storage, networks. Examples: Owner, Contributor, Reader, User Access Administrator. They are assigned through IAM on a management group, subscription, resource group or resource. The two systems are independent. The famous gap: a Global Administrator has no automatic access to Azure subscriptions. They can flip a switch, Access management for Azure resources, which elevates them to User Access Administrator at the root scope, letting them assign themselves roles. That's what question seventy nine's references hint at, the elevate access link."
</Person1><Person2> "Other Ironclad user requirements. CloudAdmin must receive email notifications about outages, and NetworkUser must create network resources."
</Person2><Person1> "Two questions you may see in the real exam about the same case study. First, outage email notifications. Service outages are Azure Service Health events. Solution: create a Service Health alert, scoped to the subscription and the services and regions, with an action group that sends email to CloudAdmin. You can also set an alert in the Service Health blade. Role not needed for email recipients, an action group can hold any address. Second, NetworkUser creating network resources in the subscription: assign Network Contributor at the subscription scope. Why not Contributor? Too broad, least privilege. Why not at a resource group? Because the requirement says within the Azure subscription, and creating new resources requires the role at the scope that will contain them. Why not Reader or Owner? Obvious."
</Person1><Person2> "A quick recap."
</Person2><Person1> "SSPR: all admin roles go through the two gate policy with no security questions. Standard users follow the configured policy, and with three questions required out of three, they answer all of them. Device settings: restrict joins to a group, set to Selected; require MFA to join, set to Yes; register is a separate matter. Service administrator is now Owner via subscription IAM. Entra roles manage the directory, Azure roles manage resources, and they are separate. Outage emails: Service Health alert with an action group. Create network resources: Network Contributor at the subscription. Next episode: RBAC role selection."
