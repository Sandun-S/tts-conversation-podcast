</Person1><Person2> "Let's take the who first."
</Person2><Person1> "There are four kinds of security principal. A user. A group. A service principal, which is the identity of an application. </Person1><Person2> "And the what is the role definition."
</Person2><Person1> "Right. A role definition is a collection of permissions, and the anatomy of it appears directly in questions sixty seven and sixty eight. </Person1><Person2> "Go."
</Person2><Person1> "Actions are control plane operations. The control plane is the management layer, Azure Resource Manager. DataActions are data plane operations, meaning what you do inside the resource, like reading the contents of a blob, or signing in to a virtual machine. NotDataActions subtract from dataActions. And assignableScopes says where this role is allowed to be assigned."
</Person1><Person2> "You said notActions are subtracted. Is that the same as a deny?"
</Person2><Person1> "This is one of the most common misunderstandings, so listen carefully. NotActions is not a deny. It is simply subtraction inside that one role. If a role has actions star and notActions Microsoft dot Authorization slash star slash write, then that role covers everything except authorization writes. But if the same person has a second role assignment that does grant authorization write, they get it. RBAC takes the union of everything granted. Real denies only come from deny assignments, which are created by Azure itself, for example by deployment stacks and managed applications, and you cannot author them directly as a normal user."
</Person1><Person2> "So RBAC is additive."
</Person2><Person1> "Completely additive. Question sixty four is built on this. </Person2><Person1> "Four levels, top to bottom. Management group, subscription, resource group, resource. </Person1><Person2> "Why NOT the action OR something? That looks backwards."

</Person1><Person2> "A formula for decoding."
