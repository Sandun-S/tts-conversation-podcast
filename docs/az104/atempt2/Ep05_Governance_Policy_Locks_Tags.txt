<Person1> "Welcome back to Study Deep Dive. Episode five: governance. Azure Policy, management groups, tags, locks, and moving resources. This covers questions twenty three to twenty eight, thirty three, sixty nine and seventy seven, and a set of topics the real exam asks about constantly."
</Person1><Person2> "Let's start by separating policy from RBAC, because you said the exam hammers that."
</Person2><Person1> "It does. Azure RBAC answers who can do what. Azure Policy answers what is allowed to exist and how resources must look. RBAC controls actions by people and identities. Policy controls the properties of resources. A person with Owner can still be blocked by a policy, because policy is evaluated on the resource request regardless of who sends it. And a policy can never grant anyone access."
</Person1><Person2> "Break down the parts of Azure Policy."
</Person2><Person1> "Four parts. A policy definition is the rule: a condition, called the if, and an effect, called the then. An initiative, also called a policy set, groups definitions together so you can assign many at once. An assignment applies a definition or initiative to a scope, with parameters. And exclusions, also called not scopes, are children of the assignment scope where it doesn't apply. There are also exemptions, which are separate objects that waive a policy for specific resources, with an expiry and a category, waiver or mitigated."
</Person1><Person2> "Walk through question sixty nine."
</Person2><Person1> "The exhibit showed an assignment. Scope CorpSub1, a subscription. Exclusion CorpSub1 slash rg finance. Definition Not allowed resource types. Parameter: Microsoft dot Storage slash storageAccounts. The question: what is the effect? Answer: you can create storage accounts in rg finance only. Trace it. The rule denies storage accounts across the assignment scope. The scope is the whole subscription, so storage accounts are blocked in every resource group. Except the excluded one, rg finance, where the policy doesn't apply. So the only place where creation succeeds is rg finance."
</Person1><Person2> "The distractors?"
</Person2><Person1> "Option one, you can create storage accounts anywhere. Ignores the policy. Option two, prevented anywhere in CorpSub1. Ignores the exclusion. Option four, prevented in rg finance only. Reverses it. And option five, you can create them anywhere but only Owners can. That mixes RBAC with policy. Policy has no concept of who. This is pattern two again, plausible sounding but from the wrong feature."
</Person1><Person2> "How could they twist it?"
</Person2><Person1> "Change exclusion to an exemption, same effect for that resource. Or change the effect from deny to audit, then you could create the storage accounts anywhere but they'd be reported non compliant. Or assign the policy at a management group and exclude a subscription. Same logic, bigger tree."
</Person1><Person2> "Explain effects in detail."
</Person2><Person1> "Deny blocks the request. Audit allows it and records non compliance. AuditIfNotExists audits when a related resource is missing, for example a VM without an agent. Append adds fields to the request, like adding a tag or an IP rule. Modify adds, updates or removes properties or tags, and can remediate existing resources through a managed identity. DeployIfNotExists deploys a related resource if it's missing, like turning on diagnostic settings, again with a managed identity and remediation tasks. Disabled turns the policy off. DenyAction blocks specific actions like delete. And there's the order of evaluation: Disabled, then Append and Modify, then Deny, then Audit."
</Person1><Person2> "When is policy evaluated?"
</Person2><Person1> "When a resource is created or updated, when an assignment is created or changed, on a regular compliance scan about every twenty four hours, and on demand through a compliance scan. Important: a Deny policy does not delete existing resources. They remain, flagged non compliant. To fix existing ones you need remediation tasks, or manual action."
</Person1><Person2> "What about built in policy examples for the exam?"
</Person2><Person1> "Allowed locations, Allowed virtual machine size SKUs, Require a tag on resources, Inherit a tag from the resource group, Not allowed resource types, Audit VMs that do not use managed disks. And custom definitions written in JSON. Another point: policy assignments inherit down the hierarchy, like RBAC."
</Person1><Person2> "Now management groups. Question thirty three had a tree."
</Person2><Person1> "Management groups let you organize subscriptions into a hierarchy and apply RBAC and Policy at that level. Facts: there's one top level root called the tenant root group, created automatically. The tree can be up to six levels deep, not counting the root level and the subscription level. A management group or subscription can have only one parent, though a management group can have many children. New subscriptions land in the default management group, the root unless you change it. You need appropriate roles to manage it, and the first time requires elevated access for a Global Administrator."
</Person1><Person2> "What can you apply at the management group level?"
</Person2><Person1> "Azure RBAC role assignments, Azure Policy assignments and initiatives, and budgets in Cost Management for some scopes. These flow down. In question thirty three, Morgan's Contributor at Global MG two covered ZenithSub, because ZenithSub was a child. And Casey's User Access Administrator at the tenant root group covered every subscription. NovaSub sat directly under the tenant root group, with no management group between. That still counts."
</Person1><Person2> "Tags next."
</Person2><Person1> "Tags are name value pairs on resources, resource groups and subscriptions, used for organization, cost tracking, automation and policy. Up to fifty tags per resource. Key facts. Tags are not inherited. A tag on a resource group does not automatically appear on its resources, and the resource's tag doesn't pass to the group either. To propagate, use Azure Policy with the Modify effect, using the built in policy Inherit a tag from the resource group, or a script. Also, not every resource type supports tags. And tags don't change permissions, though you can use tags in RBAC conditions and in policy."
</Person1><Person2> "And cost tracking?"
</Person2><Person1> "Cost Management, with budgets and alerts. A budget sends alerts, and can trigger an action group, but doesn't stop spending by itself. You can filter cost analysis by tag, so tags like CostCenter and Environment are how you charge back. Azure Advisor gives recommendations. Also reservations and savings plans reduce compute cost for committed use."
</Person1><Person2> "Resource locks."
</Person2><Person1> "Two lock levels. CanNotDelete, also written Delete, lets authorized users read and modify a resource but not delete it. ReadOnly lets authorized users only read it, no modifications, no deletion. Locks apply to everyone, even Owner, and they are inherited by child resources. A lock on a resource group applies to all resources in it, including ones added later. To manage locks you need Microsoft dot Authorization slash locks slash star, which Owner and User Access Administrator have. To delete a locked resource, first remove the lock."
</Person1><Person2> "Any gotchas?"
</Person2><Person1> "Yes, well known ones. A ReadOnly lock on a storage account blocks listing of the access keys, because listKeys is a POST operation, which the lock treats as a write. So Azure Files in the portal can fail. A ReadOnly lock on a virtual machine blocks start and restart, since those are POST. A ReadOnly lock on a resource group can stop you from creating or changing anything in it. And locks protect against accidental management plane changes, not data plane changes, so you can still write blobs in a locked storage account."
</Person1><Person2> "Question seventy seven, moving a web app."
</Person2><Person1> "Sub1 with resource groups, each linked to a region and having a policy. A web app CustomerWeb01 sits in ResGrp1 and is deployed in West Europe. You move it to ResGrp2. What happens? The App Service plan remains in West Europe, and Pol2 applies. Two facts. First, a resource group is a logical container and its location only stores metadata about the group. Moving a resource to another group doesn't change the resource's own region. Second, a move changes the parent, so the resource now inherits the new group's assignments, including policy and RBAC. The old group's assignments no longer apply."
</Person1><Person2> "The wrong options said it moves to North Europe."
</Person2><Person1> "That is the mistake: believing the group's region controls the resource. It doesn't. Resources in one group can live in different regions. To really change a resource's region, you must redeploy or use the region move process, not a resource group move."
</Person1><Person2> "What happens during a real move?"
</Person2><Person1> "The resource ID changes, because the ID includes the resource group name. Anything that uses the old ID breaks, like scripts. The move locks both the source and destination groups for writes during the operation, though resources still run. Not all resource types support moves. You can validate before moving. You can also move across subscriptions, within the same tenant, and you need write permission on the resource and the destination group. Some resources have special rules. For App Service, apps and plans in a group often must move together, which is a real world restriction you should check in the docs, but the exam answer here follows the simple principle."
</Person1><Person2> "Connect that to questions twenty three to twenty eight."
</Person2><Person1> "The maintenance puzzle had the proposals: move the VM to another subscription, to another resource group, choose One time update, click Enable on the Updates blade. All of them failed to relocate the VM to another host. Moving subscriptions or groups is only a metadata move in Azure Resource Manager. The VM keeps running on the same physical host. The only action that provisions the VM onto a new host is Redeploy, covered in the compute episode."
</Person1><Person2> "Let's cover some other governance tools: Blueprints, Resource Graph, Advisor."
</Person2><Person1> "Template specs and deployment stacks let you version and deploy infrastructure, and deployment stacks can protect resources using deny settings. Azure Resource Graph lets you query resources across subscriptions with Kusto style queries. Azure Advisor gives recommendations on reliability, security, cost, performance and operational excellence. Activity log shows management operations. Service Health tells you about Azure incidents affecting you. Resource Health tells you the health of a specific resource."
</Person1><Person2> "What about subscriptions?"
</Person2><Person1> "A subscription is a billing and access boundary. It has quotas and limits, like vCPU per region and per VM family, and you request increases through support. You can have many subscriptions under one tenant, grouped in management groups. Transferring billing ownership, moving a subscription between management groups, and changing the associated tenant are separate operations."
</Person1><Person2> "Let me list what the exam could twist about policy and moves."
</Person2><Person1> "One, effect changes from Deny to Audit. Two, exclusion versus exemption. Three, the assignment scope is a management group instead of a subscription. Four, a resource is moved and the question asks which policy applies, always the destination group. Five, the question asks which region, always unchanged. Six, a person with Owner tries to create a blocked resource, still denied by policy. Seven, someone tries to delete a resource with a lock, denied until the lock is removed."
</Person1><Person2> "Summarize."
</Person2><Person1> "Policy controls resources, RBAC controls people. Definition, initiative, assignment, exclusion. Deny blocks, Audit flags, Modify and DeployIfNotExists remediate. Management groups: up to six levels, one parent, root group at the top. Tags are not inherited. Locks beat Owner, CanNotDelete versus ReadOnly, and they inherit down. A resource group move changes the folder, not the location. Next episode: Key Vault, managed identities, and disk encryption."
</Person1>
